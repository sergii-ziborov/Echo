# ECHO player guide

ECHO is a route-planning survival game. You guide the Signal, the **bright cyan core**; violet orbs are echoes of your recorded route. Collect every spark, then reach the illuminated exit without touching an echo or a lethal hazard.

## Why you are out here

The Keepers built the Lighthouse as a reference station that let the stations of the Fold Road compare their records. After **the Break**, the recovery network began replaying recorded movement a few seconds late, and your own route became one of those replays. The Signal is the reference the Lighthouse still sends. Sparks are state records; collecting all of them confirms the route and opens the exit, a fold to the next station. Each act is a different region along the Road, so its sky, walls and hazards change with it. Every map's place and log appear in the Atlas and on the pause card, and clearing a region recovers a Keeper record in the Archive's **Story** page.

## Your first run

1. Drag anywhere on the arena to steer. The Signal seeks your finger. Lift your finger and it stops where it is, pulsing so you can find it; there is no coasting.
2. Watch the echo clock near the starting beacon. At zero, a violet echo begins replaying the route you just took. The clock is not a time limit.
3. Leave open lanes for your future self. Broad loops are safer than crossing the same point repeatedly.
4. Double-tap the arena to dash. It has its own cooldown and does **not** spend a Rewind charge.
5. Collect every spark and enter the open exit. Timed crystals are required too: take one before its ring empties for bonus Freeze time and research points. After that it still has to be collected, without the bonus.

If the Signal is lost, the screen offers **Paradox Rewind** when a charge is available. It sends you back along your recent route, and the discarded branch replays once as a lethal paradox ghost. Restart is always available.

## Reading the HUD

The top counters show sparks collected, echoes active against the map's limit, and Rewind charges. The Pause button opens restart, settings and menu actions. Skill buttons sit at the bottom; each shows its remaining charges and cooldown. A shield badge shows a hit it can still absorb.

The echo clock counts down to the next replay, while a crystal's own ring counts down only its **bonus**. Freeze pauses the systems driven by the field clock — hazards, echo playback and their countdowns, including brittle rock fracture clocks — while you keep control. When the echo limit is reached, the spawn clock disappears.

## The Signal Lab

The Lab has two tabs:

- **Skills**: unlock abilities, buy charges, and choose the limited loadout that appears during a run. You can own more skills than you can equip.
- **Research**: permanently improve the Signal and its skills in the 24-node Signal Matrix. Motion, Loadout and Timing are three related branches.

To research a node, tap it, read its current and next-rank effect, and check the listed prerequisites. Every prerequisite must reach its stated rank, and a prerequisite can sit in another branch. When the node is open and you have enough research points, press **Upgrade** in the detail panel. The numbers a node shows come from the same tuning the run applies. Slot Matrix adds skill slots, Deep Reserves raises how many charges a skill can hold, and Fast Cycle shortens cooldowns. Early Vector Drive ranks open several later branches.

Earn research points by clearing maps, earning the three optional Route Seals on each map, collecting timed crystals and chaining nearby sparks for Resonance. The Daily Rift pays its reward on the first clear of the day.

## Skills and hazards

Freeze holds the field clock while you keep moving. Surge speeds the Signal up, with no inertial coasting. Shield absorbs a hit; the obstacle stays. Magnet draws nearby sparks. Phase briefly suppresses lethal contacts, but solid walls and closed gates still block it. Pulse and Shift push the next echo back without erasing the ones already out; Anchor slows the field clock while the Signal keeps its speed; Repulse pushes nearby moving debris and clears nearby scars; Prism protects against beams; Blink transfers the Signal forward, and solid geometry limits where it lands. The Lab and the Archive show each skill's exact timing and best use.

Asteroids have distinct material shells and faint trails. Their routes vary by map: ricochets, patrols, paired orbits, and large fixed cores with smaller orbiters. Cryo Ice, Chrono Crystal and Basalt crack after wall impacts and may eventually break; Void Alloy does not. Later acts add Magma Cores, Meteoric Iron, Hollow Geodes and Comet Frost, each with its own look and toughness. Beams telegraph before firing. Field wells are local field traps, not stellar black holes: their lens marks the pull, and the dark core ends the run. Fold faults open and close: entering a calm one gives a brief hold, a collapsing one is lethal, a Warp rift moves the Signal and flips horizontal steering for a few seconds, and a Candy rift opens a short, faster training mode. These systems arrive gradually along the Road, so inspect a region and its map before entering.

## Atlas, difficulty and saves

The Fold Road Atlas holds **77 maps in 11 seven-map regions**. Swipe between regions or tap a region icon, then tap a map node to inspect it. Locked maps are shown for orientation; clear the preceding route to go on. Each map has three optional Route Seals: Clear, Control and Paradox. Finishing all 77 starts a harder named pass with its own seal and record set, while completed passes stay recorded.

Deep Time is a separate expedition of generated arenas. Each depth is checked for a connected route before it is played; it is a static check, so layouts can recur.

Progress is stored on the device. Settings changes sound volume, haptics and replay preferences, and **Language** opens the system's per-app language switch (English or Russian). **Reset progress** asks for confirmation and erases maps, difficulty, records, points, inventory and research while keeping sound and haptic preferences.

For a quick reminder inside the game, open the **Archive** from the home screen. The four-step Field Training tutorial is offered when you start a new timeline.
